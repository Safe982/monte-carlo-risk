How the simulation works

We simulate a continuously rebalanced portfolio using geometric Brownian motion. Annual portfolio drift is the weighted asset drift; variance is wᵀΣw, including correlations. Each run uses 120 time steps and seeded Gaussian shocks. This is a simplified model with no fees, taxes, contributions, or withdrawals.

Understanding VaR and expected shortfall

At 95% confidence, VaR is the loss cutoff exceeded in approximately 5% of simulated outcomes. Expected shortfall is the average loss in that worst 5%. Losses are measured against the starting balance at the selected horizon. Negative values mean gains, not losses. Neither metric is a maximum possible loss.

Assumptions, correlations, and limitations

Default annual drift / volatility: US equities 8% / 18%, international equities 7% / 20%, bonds 3.5% / 6%, gold 4.5% / 15%. Fixed correlations: US–international 0.75, US–bonds −0.10, US–gold 0.05, international–bonds −0.05, international–gold 0.10, bonds–gold 0.10. These are illustrative, editable scenarios (correlations remain fixed), not fitted historical data. Normal shocks and constant parameters can understate crashes and changing market relationships. Path-dependent drawdowns are not reported.
